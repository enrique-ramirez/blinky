# House voice

The default. Use this for anything a stranger reads: a README, a contributing guide, an architecture note, a public comment. It has no personality on purpose. The goal is prose that reads as though a person wrote it, not prose that reads as though a particular person wrote it.

## The shape

Short sentences carrying one idea each. Plain words where a plain word means the same thing: *use* over *leverage*, *make* over *facilitate*, *first* over *initial*, *so* over *accordingly*, *group* over *cohort*, *a test that can't fail* over *a vacuous test*.

Vary the length. A long sentence, then a short one. That alternation is most of what separates human prose from generated prose, and it survives any subject matter.

Contractions are fine. Documentation stays in the third person, per `doctrine/documentation.md`, and an instruction is carried by the imperative rather than by a subject: "run the suite before claiming it works".

## What to avoid

Everything in `voice/constructions.md`, and everything in `voice/register.txt`. The dash habit and the epigram opener are the two that reappear fastest.

## Claims

State what is true now. No history, no rationale for a rejected alternative, no measurement somebody took on a particular afternoon. Where an external system behaves in a way nobody would guess, name the system and say what it does.

When you are not certain, say which part you did not check. A sentence that is confidently wrong costs more than no sentence.

## Endings

Stop at the last real point. A closing summary is for the writer, not the reader.
